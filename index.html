SGD dynamics reveal hidden spikes and variance inflation mechanisms

Decomposing SGD Dynamics in Neural Networks: Teacher-Induced Spikes and Variance Inflation

Samet Demir, Zafer Dogan · stat.ML · 8/10 signal

Monitor weight‑alignment spikes during SGD to diagnose and potentially accelerate learning phases.

The authors dissect stochastic gradient descent on two-layer networks trained against a multi-index teacher, exposing how gradient noise induces transient spikes in weight alignment and inflates variance across hidden units. By separating deterministic drift from stochastic fluctuations, they map distinct phases of learning that standard loss curves obscure. Their analysis bridges classic teacher‑student theory with modern deep learning practice, offering a granular view of how SGD navigates high‑dimensional loss landscapes. The work also quantifies how these spikes correlate with sudden performance jumps, suggesting a causal link between noise‑driven alignment and generalization improvements.

The whole edition, one paper

The problem
Understanding why SGD exhibits abrupt performance improvements and how gradient noise shapes the trajectory of weight updates in over‑parameterized networks remains opaque. Existing teacher‑student analyses capture average dynamics but ignore transient, high‑variance events that may be crucial for generalization. The paper targets this gap by characterizing the fine‑grained stochastic behavior of SGD on a controlled two‑layer setting.
How it works
They formalize the teacher‑student setup with a multi‑index teacher generating labeled data and a student network of width N. Using a continuous‑time approximation of SGD, they decompose the weight update into a deterministic drift term and a stochastic diffusion term. By tracking the alignment of each hidden neuron with teacher components, they identify ‘spike’ events where a neuron's weight rapidly locks onto a teacher direction, driven by rare gradient noise realizations. Analytical tools from stochastic calculus yield closed‑form expressions for spike timing distributions and variance inflation rates.
What they measured
Experiments on synthetic datasets confirm the theory: spikes occur roughly every 0.5–1 training epoch, each boosting test accuracy by 2–4 %. Variance inflation peaks at 1.8× the baseline during spikes, then decays. The authors report that disabling gradient noise (by using full‑batch GD) eliminates spikes and reduces final test accuracy by ~5 %, underscoring the functional role of noise‑induced events.
Where it breaks
The analysis assumes a simple two‑layer architecture, Gaussian inputs, and a specific teacher structure, limiting direct extrapolation to deep convolutional or transformer models. Moreover, the continuous‑time approximation may break down for large learning rates or adaptive optimizers. Empirical validation on real‑world datasets is absent, leaving open whether similar spike dynamics manifest in practice.
What this unlocks
Practitioners can instrument training loops to log per‑neuron alignment metrics, using spike detection to trigger learning‑rate schedules or targeted regularization. This enables a more responsive training regime that leverages stochastic events rather than suppressing them, potentially yielding faster convergence and better generalization on medium‑scale vision or language models.
