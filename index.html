T2I generation succeeds without massive language pretraining

Text-to-Image Generative Modeling Without Language Pretraining

Keshigeyan Chandrasegaran, Yue Zhao, Armin W. Thomas · cs.LG · 8/10 signal

You can drop frozen language backbones and still get state‑of‑the‑art T2I quality if you train cross‑modal attention end‑to‑end on enough paired data.

The authors challenge the prevailing belief that large-scale language models are a prerequisite for high-fidelity text‑to‑image synthesis. By training a diffusion model directly on paired image‑text data, they demonstrate comparable FID and CLIP‑Score to baselines that rely on frozen language encoders. The work isolates the contribution of language pretraining and shows that visual grounding can be learned end‑to‑end when sufficient paired data is available.

Crucially, the paper introduces a lightweight tokenizer that operates on subword fragments and aligns them with visual patches via cross‑modal attention. This design eliminates the need for external language embeddings, reducing compute overhead by ~30% and enabling tighter integration between text and image latent spaces.

The whole edition, one paper

The problem
Modern T2I pipelines depend on massive language models pretrained on billions of tokens, inflating training cost and limiting flexibility for domain‑specific vocabularies. The authors ask whether this dependency is essential for high‑quality generation or merely a convenience.
How it works
They construct a unified diffusion backbone that ingests raw token sequences produced by a byte‑pair encoding (BPE) tokenizer trained on the same image‑text corpus. Cross‑modal attention layers fuse token embeddings with image patch embeddings at every diffusion step, allowing the model to learn joint semantics from scratch. A contrastive loss aligns text and image latents, while classifier‑free guidance is applied using a learned unconditional token.
What they measured
On MS‑COCO 2014, the model attains FID 7.9 and CLIP‑Score 0.32, within 5% of a baseline that uses a frozen CLIP text encoder. Training cost drops from 2.5k GPU‑hours to 1.8k GPU‑hours. Ablations show that removing the cross‑modal attention degrades FID to 12.4, confirming its central role.
Where it breaks
The approach still requires a large, high‑quality paired dataset; performance degrades sharply on low‑resource domains. The BPE tokenizer lacks the semantic richness of transformer‑based language models, limiting nuanced compositional prompts. Moreover, the paper does not evaluate zero‑shot generalization to out‑of‑distribution text.
What this unlocks
Practitioners can now train domain‑specific T2I models without sourcing or licensing massive language backbones, cutting both memory footprint and inference latency. This enables rapid prototyping of specialized generators—e.g., medical imaging reports or industrial design sketches—where custom vocabularies dominate.
