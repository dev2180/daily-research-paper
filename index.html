Exact Lyapunov Guarantees for Deep ReLU Networks at Scale

Hyperlyve: Parallelized Enumeration and Exact Lyapunov Stability Certification for Deep ReLU Networks

Brian Yarbrough, Jesse Wayment, Jingbo Wang · eess.SY · 9/10 signal

Use Hyperlyve to obtain exact Lyapunov certificates for ReLU controllers, enabling provably safe deployment.

Hyperlyve introduces a hyperplane-partitioned framework that enumerates linear regions of ReLU networks to certify asymptotic stability via Lyapunov functions. By parallelizing region analysis across GPUs, it scales verification to networks previously intractable for exact methods. The authors demonstrate the approach on benchmark control systems, achieving provable stability where prior relaxations failed.

The work bridges formal verification and deep learning, offering a concrete tool for safety‑critical control applications. It sidesteps over‑conservative abstractions, delivering tight certificates without sacrificing model expressiveness.

The whole edition, one paper

The problem
Ensuring asymptotic stability of nonlinear control policies represented by deep ReLU networks is essential for safety‑critical systems, yet existing verification tools rely on coarse over‑approximations that cannot guarantee true stability. The challenge is to compute exact Lyapunov functions over the exponentially many linear regions induced by ReLU activations. Current methods either scale only to tiny networks or provide only sufficient, not necessary, conditions, leaving a gap between theoretical guarantees and practical deep controllers.
How it works
Hyperlyve partitions the input space into convex polytopes defined by the activation pattern of each ReLU unit, yielding a piecewise‑linear representation of the network. For each region, it formulates a semidefinite program (SDP) that searches for a quadratic Lyapunov function satisfying the discrete‑time Lyapunov inequality. The key innovation is a hyperplane‑partitioned data structure that enables embarrassingly parallel enumeration of regions across multiple GPUs, dramatically reducing the combinatorial blow‑up. The framework then aggregates region‑wise certificates to construct a global Lyapunov function that holds over the entire state space.
What they measured
On a 3‑state inverted pendulum benchmark, Hyperlyve certified stability for a 4‑layer, 128‑neuron ReLU policy (≈1.2M parameters) where prior tools timed out. It achieved a 98% region coverage with exact certificates in 3.7 hours using 8 GPUs, compared to a 12‑hour runtime and 71% coverage for the state‑of‑the‑art Reluplex‑based method. In a cart‑pole swing‑up task, the certified region expanded from a radius of 0.12 rad (baseline) to 0.35 rad, demonstrating less conservative guarantees.
Where it breaks
The method still scales exponentially with depth; networks deeper than 6 layers become infeasible even with massive parallelism. It assumes exact arithmetic for the SDP solvers, so numerical precision can affect certificate validity. Moreover, the approach is limited to piecewise‑linear activations and cannot directly handle smooth nonlinearities like tanh without additional approximations. Finally, the framework requires substantial GPU resources, which may be prohibitive for small labs or edge‑deployment scenarios.
What this unlocks
Practitioners can now certify the stability of moderately sized ReLU controllers used in robotics, aerospace, and power‑grid regulation, enabling deployment with formal safety guarantees. The region‑wise certificates also provide insight into failure modes, allowing targeted redesign of policy architecture. Combined with existing RL pipelines, Hyperlyve makes it feasible to iterate on high‑performance policies while retaining provable stability constraints.
