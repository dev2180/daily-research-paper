Self‑attention can self‑organize into chaotic and localized regimes without supervision

Nonequilibrium Phases of Repulsive Self-Attention: Chaos, Attention Condensation, and Emergent Locality

Qucheng Gao, Zuyi Yang, Xiao Chen · cond-mat.dis-nn · 9/10 signal

Use repulsive self‑attention to induce sparse, locality‑preserving representations in recurrent transformers.

The authors expose a gap in our theoretical understanding of transformers: most analyses assume stable, trained attention, yet the intrinsic dynamics of self‑attention remain opaque. By constructing a stripped‑down recurrent transformer where queries and keys are identity matrices and values are the negative identity, they create a purely repulsive attention mechanism that can be studied analytically. They then map the nonequilibrium behavior of this system, showing that it does not settle into a fixed point but exhibits distinct dynamical phases. Empirical simulations corroborate the theory, revealing regimes of chaos, attention condensation, and emergent locality that arise purely from the sign of the value matrix.
