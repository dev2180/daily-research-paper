CausalArena: Benchmarking Causal Discovery in the Foundation Model Era

Zi-Rong Li, Si-Yang Liu, Tian-Zuo Wang · cs.LG · 9/10 signal

Incorporate foundation‑model features when training causal discovery pipelines to gain measurable accuracy gains on realistic data.

CausalArena addresses the chronic gap between synthetic causal benchmarks and real‑world complexity by assembling a suite of datasets that span pure simulations, language‑model‑generated graphs, and domain‑specific tabular collections. The benchmark standardizes preprocessing, intervention protocols, and evaluation metrics, enabling apples‑to‑apples comparison of causal discovery algorithms at scale.

The authors evaluate a spectrum of classic and neural methods—GES, NOTEARS, DAG‑GNN, and a new foundation‑model‑augmented variant—showing that leveraging large‑language‑model embeddings can cut structural Hamming distance by up to 45% on high‑dimensional tasks. All code, data, and a Docker‑based evaluation harness are released under an MIT license.

The whole edition, one paper

The problem
Current causal discovery research is hamstrung by benchmarks that are either overly simplistic synthetic graphs or narrowly scoped real datasets, making it hard to assess robustness of algorithms in the foundation‑model era. Practitioners need a unified, scalable testbed that reflects the high‑dimensional, noisy, and partially observed data encountered in modern applications.
How it works
CausalArena curates 12 datasets ranging from classic synthetic DAGs (linear Gaussian, non‑linear additive) to graphs generated by prompting large language models and to real‑world tabular corpora (e.g., healthcare, finance). Each dataset comes with a predefined intervention set and a Docker‑encapsulated evaluation pipeline that computes SHD, F1, AUROC, and runtime under identical conditions. A novel baseline augments node features with frozen embeddings from a 7B LLM, feeding them into NOTEARS‑style continuous optimization.
What they measured
On the LLM‑generated graphs (average 150 nodes), GES achieved SHD 27, NOTEARS 22, and the embedding‑augmented NOTEARS 12—a 45% reduction. On the real‑world healthcare set (80 variables), DAG‑GNN’s F1 rose from 0.58 to 0.71 when using LLM features. Across all benchmarks, runtime overhead for embedding augmentation was under 15% thanks to batched inference.
Where it breaks
The suite focuses on tabular and graph‑structured data; image‑oriented causal tasks remain unsupported. Interventions are synthetic for most LLM‑generated graphs, so external validity for truly causal mechanisms is unproven. Scalability beyond ~500 variables has not been demonstrated, and the embedding‑based baseline relies on a frozen LLM, limiting adaptability to domain‑specific vocabularies.
What this unlocks
Practitioners can now stress‑test their causal discovery pipelines against a realistic spectrum of problems without hand‑crafting datasets. The provided Docker harness lets teams benchmark new algorithms quickly, and the embedding‑augmented baseline offers a ready‑to‑use recipe for boosting performance on high‑dimensional, noisy data.
