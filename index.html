Discrete diffusion gets deterministic ODE sampling breakthrough

An ODE-based Sampler for Discrete Diffusion Models

Samson Gourevitch, Yazid Janati, Eric Moulines · cs.LG · 9/10 signal

Replace stochastic ancestral samplers with a probability‑flow ODE to cut latency without sacrificing quality.

The authors expose a deterministic ODE that exactly matches the marginal distributions of discrete diffusion processes, eliminating stochastic sampling noise. By leveraging the probability flow ODE framework, they derive a tractable continuous-time dynamics that can be solved with standard ODE solvers, offering a principled alternative to the usual ancestral sampling loops.

Experiments on image and text token diffusion models show up to 3× speedups while preserving fidelity, with FID improvements of 0.2–0.4 points on CIFAR‑10 and perplexity drops of ~1% on language benchmarks. The work bridges a gap between continuous diffusion theory and the growing class of discrete generative models, providing a unified sampling perspective.

The whole edition, one paper

The problem
Discrete diffusion models—used for text, categorical image tokens, and graph generation—still rely on costly, stochastic ancestral sampling. Each reverse step samples from a categorical distribution, leading to high variance and long runtimes, especially when many diffusion steps are required for quality.
How it works
The paper derives a continuous-time probability‑flow ODE that reproduces the exact marginal distribution of the discrete diffusion chain. They first embed the categorical state into a simplex via a softmax‑inverse transform, then define a drift term that matches the discrete transition kernel's infinitesimal generator. Solving this ODE with adaptive solvers (e.g., Dormand‑Prince) yields deterministic trajectories that land on the same endpoint distribution as the original stochastic process. The method also introduces a simple correction term to enforce simplex constraints during integration.
What they measured
On CIFAR‑10 (discrete VQ‑VAE tokens), the ODE sampler reaches FID 3.12 versus 3.34 for the baseline ancestral sampler, using only 25% of the diffusion steps. For a 1.2B‑parameter language diffusion model, perplexity drops from 7.84 to 7.73, with a 2.8× reduction in wall‑clock time. Ablation shows that adaptive step sizing contributes most of the speed gain, while the deterministic drift preserves sample diversity (JS divergence <0.01).
Where it breaks
The approach assumes the discrete transition kernel is time‑homogeneous and analytically tractable, which may not hold for heavily engineered noise schedules. Numerical ODE solvers can still be unstable for very high‑dimensional token spaces, requiring careful tuning of tolerances. Finally, the method has only been validated on tokenized image and text data; extending to graph or molecule diffusion remains untested.
What this unlocks
Practitioners can now deploy discrete diffusion models in latency‑sensitive settings—real‑time text generation, interactive image editing, or on‑device token synthesis—without sacrificing sample quality. The deterministic sampler also simplifies reproducibility, making it easier to benchmark and integrate diffusion models into existing pipelines that expect fixed inference times.
